\chapter*{Abstract}
{\LARGE
\noindent\textbf{Title:} HDO VideoAPI: A Provider-Based Middleware for
Unified Access to Multiple Video Engines in Norwegian Emergency Medicine
}

\bigskip

In Norwegian emergency medicine, video streaming is used as decision support
for \gls{amk} and \gls{lvs} operators. The video solution is operated by \gls{hdo}
and is built on two separate video engines that are technically dissimilar, which
complicates unified access for client applications.

This thesis investigates to what extent a \gls{provider}-based middleware
architecture can expose a single common \gls{api} to clients, while logic specific
to each video engine is kept isolated from the core. The assessment is made along
three dimensions: API coverage, backend isolation, and non-functional requirements.
Limitations arising from heterogeneous domain models are discussed as part of this.

The project was carried out as a design and implementation project with an
iterative working process. The solution was evaluated through requirements
traceability, automated tests, end-to-end testing against HDO's test
environments, architectural validation in which two additional providers were
added, and a structured final evaluation from the client.

The results show that the architecture covers the core operations of creating,
retrieving, and deleting video rooms across both engines, and that backend-specific
logic can largely be isolated within the provider layer. Full standardization is
nevertheless difficult when the engines are built on different domain models ---
this applies in particular to starting and stopping video streams, where the
semantics are not comparable between the two engines. The non-functional
requirements for observability, logging, and operability are also met.

The conclusion is that a provider-based middleware is suitable for HDO's
proof-of-concept context. For the solution to be taken into production, further
development is required on two fronts: security, in the form of a stricter
authorization model and production-ready \gls{token} validation; and robustness,
in the form of load testing and more systematic handling of error states in
external video engines.
